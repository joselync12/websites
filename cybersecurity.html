My Cybersecurity Awareness

Protecting your digital mission

Reflection Questions

What does cybersecurity mean to me?

To me, cybersecurity means protecting people and their information in an increasingly digital world. It's not just about firewalls and encryption — it's about being aware of threats like phishing, making smart choices online, and understanding that human awareness is the first line of defense. Cybersecurity is about staying vigilant so that technology remains a tool for good, not a source of harm.

Why is cybersecurity important?

Cybersecurity is important because everyone is a potential target. Phishing attacks, data breaches, and identity theft affect millions of people every year. A single careless click can lead to stolen passwords, financial loss, or compromised personal information. As we rely more on digital tools for work, school, and daily life, protecting ourselves online is just as important as locking our doors in the physical world.

What is one cybersecurity habit I practice?

One habit I practice is always verifying before I click. I hover over links to preview the actual URL, check sender email addresses carefully, and never download attachments from unknown sources. If something feels off — like an urgent request for personal information — I take a moment to verify through a trusted channel before taking any action.

What cybersecurity topic do I want to learn this semester?

This semester, I want to learn more about how phishing campaigns are structured and how organizations detect and respond to them. I'm curious about the psychology behind social engineering, the technical indicators that reveal a phishing attempt, and the tools security teams use to protect their users. Understanding this will make me a more aware and responsible digital citizen.

Phishing Awareness: Quick Tips

Here are some key ways to protect yourself from phishing attacks:

  • Check the sender's email address — look for misspellings or suspicious domains
  • Hover over links — preview the URL before clicking
  • Be wary of urgency — phishing often creates false urgency to rush you
  • Never share passwords via email — legitimate organizations won't ask for this
  • Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) — adds an extra layer of security
  • When in doubt, verify — contact the organization through a trusted channel
